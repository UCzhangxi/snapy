# snapy #250, D2 literature review: flux globalization and well-balanced references

Page conventions:
- **Chertock+2018**: pages of arXiv:1712.08218v1 (21 Dec 2017, 24 pp), the submitted version found through Unpaywall. I did not get the JCP 358:36–52 version, so JCP page numbers are not given. The equation numbers are the arXiv ones.
- **Käppeli2022**: printed article pages ("Page N of 88") of Living Rev. Comput. Astrophys. 8:2 (2022), taken from the published open-access PDF in the ETH Research Collection. The PDF adds one cover page, so PDF page = printed page + 1.

Lines marked *[inference]* are my reading. They are **not** stated in either text.

## (a) Boundaries: the anchor and the ghost cells

- D2 Chertock+2018 p.2, eq.(1.4): the global variables Q = ∫^x ρ φ_x dξ and R = ∫^y ρ φ_y dη are printed with only an upper limit. The continuous formulation leaves the integration constant open.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.5, eq.(2.8): in 1-D the constant is set at the bottom boundary, R_{kℓ−1/2} = 0. Interface values then follow from an upward midpoint recursion, R_{k+1/2} = R_{k−1/2} + Δy ρ̄_k (φ_y)_k, for k = kℓ…kr.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.6, eqs.(2.12)–(2.13): the cell-centre R_k is the average of the two interface values, and L_k = p_k + R_k. L is reconstructed with minmod. The face pressures are recovered as p^{N,S} = L^{N,S} − R_{k±1/2}.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.9, eq.(3.2): in 2-D, Q_{jℓ−1/2,k} = 0 on the left edge and R_{j,kℓ−1/2} = 0 on the bottom edge, for every row and column. Each is a 1-D running sum along its own direction.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.21, App. B.2: to build 2-D discrete steady states, they set Q = 0 on the right edge and R = 0 on the bottom edge. They add "alternatively, one could set the zero values … at the left and upper parts". Which edge carries the anchor is a free choice.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.11, Example 1: reflecting boundaries use ghost cells: ρ copied, v negated, L copied (L_{kℓ−1} := L_{kℓ}, L_{kr+1} := L_{kr}).
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.13, Example 2 (isothermal): zero-order extrapolation of v and L. The ghost density is ρ_{kℓ−1} = ρ_{kℓ} e^{Δy(φ_y)_{kℓ}} and ρ_{kr+1} = ρ_{kr} e^{−Δy(φ_y)_{kr}}. The L condition is equivalent to discrete hydrostatic ghost pressures, p_{kℓ−1} = p_{kℓ} + Δy ρ_{kℓ}(φ_y)_{kℓ} and p_{kr+1} = p_{kr} − Δy ρ_{kr}(φ_y)_{kr}.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.15, Example 3: same boundary conditions as Example 2.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.16, Example 4: zero-order extensions at all four edges.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.17, Example 5: zero-order extrapolation in all directions.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.68: boundary conditions are "a delicate and strongly application-dependent matter". Typically the last physical cell's local equilibrium profile is extrapolated into the ghost cells.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.69, eq.(217): ghost averages are U_k = Q_k(U_eq,1) for k<1 and Q_k(U_eq,N) for k>N. The alternative is to freeze the ghost data at the initial equilibrium. In multi-D, boundaries are applied direction by direction.

## (b) Block seams

- D2 Käppeli2022 p.64: in Chertock+2018 the global fluxes "essentially correspond to an integration of the hydrostatic pressure over the whole computational domain".
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.5, eq.(2.8); p.9, eq.(3.2): R_{k+1/2} is a prefix sum from the anchored edge. R in any cell depends on ρ̄ in every cell between it and the domain edge.
- D2 Chertock+2018 pp.7–8, Theorem 2.1 proof: the energy flux vanishes at rest only because both one-sided pressures at face k+1/2 subtract the same interface value, R_{k+1/2}. The text reads (β/(2(γ−1)))[(L^S_{k+1} − R_{k+1/2}) − (L^N_k − R_{k+1/2})] = 0. The momentum flux is constant because L^N_k = L^S_{k+1} = L̂ everywhere.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.7, eq.(2.15)–(2.16), text below: the cut-off argument ψ is normalised by (y_{kr+1/2} − y_{kℓ−1/2}) / max{L_k, L_{k+1}}. This uses the full domain length and L itself, and L depends on where R is anchored.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.10, below (3.4): in 2-D the normaliser is printed as (x_{jr+1/2} − x_{jℓ−1/2}) / max_{j,k}{K_{j,k}, K_{j+1,k}}, and likewise for L. As printed, the max is subscripted j,k, which reads as a domain-wide max. The subscripts on ψ also look swapped (ψ_{j,k+1/2} is written with K).
- D2 Chertock+2018 (whole text) and D2 Käppeli2022 (whole text): neither discusses MPI, domain decomposition or multi-block meshes. A text search for parallel, MPI, processor and decomposition finds nothing relevant.
- *[inference]* A decomposed code needs an exclusive prefix scan along x1 over the stack of blocks: each block's column sum of Δy ρ φ_y, offset by the sum of every block below it (or above it, for a top anchor). One constant offset shared by all blocks is harmless, because p = L − R is gauge-invariant. What breaks the scheme is a block-local offset. Then the two sides of a seam face see different R_{k+1/2}, and the momentum flux L^N vs L^S jumps by the offset difference. That violates the equality used in the Theorem 2.1 proof, and well-balancing fails at the seam.

## (c) Top anchor and local forms

- D2 Chertock+2018 p.5, eq.(2.8); p.9, eq.(3.2): the paper's anchor is at the bottom (lower) edge, not the top. The only other anchor mentioned is the alternative at p.21, App. B.2.
- D2 Chertock+2018 and D2 Käppeli2022: neither text defines a local (per-cell or per-block) version of the flux-globalization scheme, and neither analyses dropping its anchor.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.52, §3.3: "any barotropic or discrete steady states type scheme can be reduced to an a priori type scheme by simply freezing the piecewise steady reconstruction". The text says categories 2 and 3, not only 3.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.62, §3.3.5, and p.64: Chertock+2018 is listed under category 3, discrete steady states. It is described as second-order central-upwind with global fluxes and a smooth cut-off on the viscosity near steady state. Related work named there: Gascón & Corberán 2001 and Caselles+2009. p.64 has only this one bullet on the global-flux approach; there is no longer summary of the family.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.65, eq.(205): the category-3 equilibrium is p_eq(x) = p0 − ∫_{x0}^{x} ρ_eq dφ/dx dx, with p0 given at "some reference coordinate x0".
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.65, eq.(209) and fn. 21: the local form (Käppeli & Mishra 2016; Berberich+2021b) anchors each cell's pressure profile at its own centre with a point value p0,i. "Any other anchor point is possible."
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.66, eq.(211): p0,i is fixed per cell by matching the cell-average internal energy, a scalar Newton solve (analytic for 2nd order or an ideal gas). No domain-level constant is needed.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.67, eq.(214): in multi-D the line-integral construction is path-dependent. It is unique only in special cases, "such as constant density or alignment of gravity forces with one coordinate axis". Otherwise it needs a vanishing discrete curl of ρ∇φ.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.68: "Although not well-balanced in a multi-dimensional sense", the scheme "considerably improves the simulation of nearly hydrostatic configurations".
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.61, eqs.(200)–(201), fn. 18: the barotropic category also anchors at the cell centre. The two anchors (ϑ0,i, H0,i) come per cell from the density and energy averages. "Any anchor location is fine."
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.58, eq.(191): an isentropic equilibrium is h + φ = const. "Once the constant [is] fixed at some reference coordinate, the hydrostatic stratification is fully determined."
- *[inference]* In Chertock's scheme a uniform shift of R cancels in p = L − R and in the rest-state proof. Dropping or moving the anchor therefore does not by itself break exact well-balancing, as long as every face uses one consistent R. It does change L, and so the cut-off normaliser max{L} (p.7) and the size of the cancellation noted at pp.18–19.

## Relevant to a rho_ref for snapy x1 reconstruction

- D2 Chertock+2018 p.6, §2.2: ρ and ρv keep the ordinary limited piecewise-linear reconstruction. Only the energy is replaced by the equilibrium variable L = p + R. The well-balance comes from the pressure-like variable, not from a density reference.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.6, Remark 2.1: for linear φ, integrating the piecewise-linear ρ gives a piecewise-quadratic R. Then R_k = gΔy Σ_{i<k} ρ̄_i + gΔy ρ̄_k/2 − g(Δy)²(ρ_y)_k/8, so the density slope enters the pressure reference.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.7, eqs.(2.15)–(2.16): the numerical-diffusion terms in ρ and in ρφ are multiplied by H(ψ) = (Cψ)^m/(1+(Cψ)^m) with C = 200 and m = 6. H → 0 when |L_{k+1} − L_k| is locally ~0. At rest the density diffusion is switched off rather than the density being reconstructed exactly.
- D2 Chertock+2018 pp.18–19, §5: when ρφ, Q or R are orders of magnitude larger than p and E, recovering p from K = p + Q, L = p + R and E + ρφ "may … lead to inaccurate calculations". The authors leave this to separate study.
- D2 Chertock+2018 p.16: the well-balanced scheme costs 25–40% more than the non-well-balanced one on the same grid.
- D2 Käppeli2022 pp.19–20, eqs.(46)–(48): the reconstruction is U_eq,i + δU_i. The perturbation is formed by extrapolating cell i's own equilibrium to its neighbours' averages. The perturbation vanishes on a steady state of interest.
- D2 Käppeli2022 pp.19–20: if no local equilibrium matches the cell average, U_eq,i is set to 0 and the reconstruction falls back to the standard one.
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.66, eq.(210), and p.67, Fig. 7: the equilibrium density is by default the standard reconstruction of ρ, and "other choices are possible". The choice sets the equilibrium accuracy and stencil. With a (2k+1)-point density stencil, the pressure profile is observed at order 2(k+1).
- D2 Käppeli2022 p.52: freezing a dynamic reconstruction gives an a-priori scheme. In #250's terms, a fixed reference (bottom-anchored isentrope at t=0) is a frozen special case of a dynamic one (smooth5, local polytrope).
- *[inference]* Put p.61 (the per-cell barotropic anchor) next to p.58 (one constant sets the whole isentrope): a single bottom-cell isentrope, as in athena's default, is the global-anchor case, and Käppeli's barotropic schemes re-anchor in every cell. This matches #250 T1, where the bottom-anchored isentrope fails on tall isothermal columns and the per-cell local polytrope does not.
- *[inference]* With Chertock's bottom anchor, L ≈ p_bottom and R ≈ p_bottom − p. Near the top, p ≪ L, so p = L − R loses digits. A top anchor keeps |R| ≲ p through the column. This is the cancellation of pp.18–19 seen from the anchor side.
